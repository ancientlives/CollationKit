CollationKit

Scholarly textual collation for editions and translations. It finds every insertion, deletion, substitution and moved passage, and cites each one by page, line and word.

Try the demos View on GitHub Research introduction

What it is

Scholars who study a work that survives in several forms (a manuscript, corrected proofs, a first edition, a revised edition, rival translations) need to know precisely how those versions differ. This is textual collation, and its product is an apparatus criticus: a citable record of every variant.

CollationKit is a deterministic, pure-Swift engine for that job. Given several witnesses of one work, it reports each difference as a typed variant, including passages that moved across a page boundary. Every finding carries a citation. It separates substantive changes of wording from accidentals (spelling, punctuation, case). It merges many witnesses into one variant graph, with or without a privileged base text, and can anchor witnesses across languages.

It ships with the collate command-line tool, a self-contained interactive HTML viewer, a language-neutral conformance corpus with a JSON Schema, and complete novels in rival translations for evaluation.

Why not diff?

Words, not lines

Prose reflows and revision happens at the level of the phrase. Needleman–Wunsch alignment over tokens makes a reworded clause one substitution, not a deleted block plus an inserted one.

Moves are moves

A passage relocated across a page, even one revised in transit, is reported once as a transposition, with certain or likely confidence.

Not every difference matters

Substantive variants are separated from accidentals, so a GB/US spelling doesn't drown the finding. Accidentals can still be recorded for a diplomatic view.

A citation, not a patch

Every variant is located as p. 2 · line 1 · words 1–10, under an explicit pagination model.

Example

From the located variant report for the bundled six-edition example, comparing the manuscript (MS) with the Uniform edition (UNI):

$ collate run MS.txt UNI.txt
MS vs UNI: 5 variant(s)
  [1] TRANSPOSITION (moved)  (ACROSS PAGES)
      MS:  “The lamps were lit along the quay one by one” @ p.2 · line 1 · words 1–10
      UNI: “The lamps were lit along the quay one by one” @ p.1 · line 2 · words 1–10
  …
  [4] SUBSTITUTION
      MS:  “tired”  @ p.1 · line 2 · word 3
      UNI: “weary”  @ p.1 · line 3 · word 3
  [5] SUBSTITUTION
      MS:  “cold”   @ p.2 · line 2 · word 2
      UNI: “bitter” @ p.2 · line 1 · word 2

A line diff would report the moved sentence twice: as a deletion on page 2 and an unrelated insertion on page 1.

Get started

Requires macOS 13 or later and Swift 5.9+ (Xcode 15 or later). No third-party dependencies.

git clone https://github.com/ancientlives/CollationKit.git
cd CollationKit
swift test                # 222 tests
swift build -c release    # use release builds for full-length texts
.build/release/collate run --dir corpus/verne/journey-centre-earth --all --out results
open results/collation.html
collate                                         # interactive menu
collate run base.txt witness.txt …              # scriptable
collate run --strategy peer-msa --lexicon fr-en.lex fr.txt en1.txt en2.txt

Research

The deterministic core is mature and has been evaluated on complete novels in independent translations, on verse, and on non-Latin scripts. The open questions sit above it and around it.

A semantic layer, carefully gated
Can learned signals such as sentence-level cross-language anchoring or phrase commonness sharpen alignment and move confidence, while the engine stays deterministic, auditable and faithful to each witness's words?
The irreducible residual
Short passages near their original position can't be told from coincidence by geometry alone. Word and phrase rarity have already been shown not to help. What can?
Visualisation and trust
Which views do scholars actually rely on? Does the alignment map let a reader verify an alignment? How should calibrated uncertainty be shown to an expert entitled to overrule the tool?

Two papers are in preparation, one on the algorithms and their evaluation and one on visualising collation results. The research introduction gives the full picture.

Contributing

Code, evaluation texts, bug reports and research questions are all welcome through pull requests. Read CONTRIBUTING.md for the workflow and the engine's invariants. A collation that looks wrong on a real text is useful evaluation data, so please open an issue.
